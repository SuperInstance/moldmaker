## The bilge alarm

So the thing is the bilge alarm went off again last night, third time
this month, and every time it's nothing, it's just the float switch
sticking. But one of these times it's not going to be nothing.

> ▸ **Meant:** a reliability complaint that is really a trust
> complaint. The alarm's false-positive rate has destroyed its
> signal value. Connects to the user's standing principle (record,
> 2026-10-06): a gauge is trusted because a human witnessed it work;
> an alarm that cries wolf has no witness value left. Action
> candidate for the logic agent: spec a two-sensor agreement rule
> (two independent gauges agreeing = trust) before any hardware
> change is proposed.

## What I actually want

What I actually want is to know, when it goes off at 3 a.m., whether
the pump is *running*, not whether the switch *thinks* it should be
running. Go check the thing itself, not the story about the thing.

> ▸ **Meant (formalized):** requirement — alarm state must be
> derived from measured pump current draw, not from float-switch
> state. Inference, high confidence, stated by the user almost
> verbatim in the next sentence. This becomes a spec line in the
> twin (§actions[0]) and a question for the hardware agent: what
> current sensor fits the existing pump circuit? — *not* a question
> for the user; he has already answered the part that is his.
